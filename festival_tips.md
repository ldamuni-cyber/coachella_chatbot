# Coachella Festival Tips & Knowledge Base

## Getting There & Parking
- Coachella is held at the Empire Polo Club in Indio, California.
- The nearest major airport is Palm Springs International (PSP), about 20 minutes away. LAX is 2.5 hours away.
- Shuttles are available from multiple LA-area locations and are highly recommended over driving. Shuttle passes typically cost $75-$100 round trip.
- If driving, carpooling is essential. Parking passes sell out — purchase in advance. General parking is $40/day; preferred is $125+/day.
- Rideshares (Uber/Lyft) are available but surge pricing can reach $80-$150+ late at night.
- On-site camping (Car Camping, Tent Camping, Lake Eldorado) sells out fast and must be purchased with your ticket.

## Tickets & Wristbands
- General Admission (GA) weekend passes range from $549–$599 (Weekend 1) and slightly less for Weekend 2.
- VIP passes range from $1,199–$1,399 and include dedicated viewing areas, air-conditioned lounges, private bars, and upgraded restrooms.
- GA+ is a mid-tier option (~$849) with some VIP perks like lounge access.
- Wristbands are mailed in advance and must be registered on the Coachella app to your account.
- Lost wristbands cannot be replaced — guard yours carefully.

## What to Bring
Must-have items:
- Reusable water bottle (refill stations are free throughout the grounds)
- Sunscreen (SPF 50+) — the desert sun is brutal, especially in the afternoon
- Comfortable, broken-in shoes — you walk 5-10 miles per day
- Light layers — it can be 95°F during the day and 55°F at night
- Portable phone charger / power bank
- Small backpack or fanny pack
- Cash and credit cards (both accepted at most vendors)
- A physical copy of your ID in addition to a photo
- Bandana or buff for dust storms

Do NOT bring:
- Outside food or large coolers (small sealed snacks ok)
- Professional cameras with detachable lenses
- Selfie sticks
- Umbrellas (they block others' views)
- Glass containers
- Drones

## Food & Drinks
- Food vendors are spread across the grounds. Expect to spend $15-$25 per meal.
- Popular options include gourmet food trucks, plant-based options, and local restaurant pop-ups.
- Alcohol is sold throughout the venue. A beer averages $12-$15; cocktails $16-$20.
- Water refill stations are free and located near all major stages.
- The Rose Garden area has upscale dining options at a premium price.
- Budget tip: Eat a solid meal at camp before entering the festival each day.

## Stages & Navigation
There are 7 main stages:
1. **Main Stage (Coachella Stage)** — Largest stage, headliners close each night. Can hold 100,000+.
2. **Outdoor Theatre** — Second largest, slightly more intimate feel, great for mid-tier headliners.
3. **Sahara Tent** — Electronic and dance music hub. Massive LED production.
4. **Mojave Tent** — Rock and alternative acts. Great sound quality.
5. **Gobi Tent** — Emerging artists and indie acts. Less crowded.
6. **Sonora Tent** — Latin music focus. Incredible energy.
7. **Yuma** — Underground electronic and techno. Small capacity, arrives early to get in.

Navigation tips:
- Download the official Coachella app for interactive maps and live schedules.
- Give yourself 15-20 minutes to walk between far stages (Main Stage to Yuma is a long walk).
- Crowds are thickest during headliner changeovers (9-10pm). Plan accordingly.
- Sahara gets extremely packed for big electronic acts — arrive 45 minutes early.

## Health & Safety
- Stay hydrated — drink at least 2-3 liters of water per day in the desert heat.
- Know the location of medical stations (marked on the app map).
- Heat exhaustion is common: symptoms include dizziness, nausea, and stopping sweating. Seek shade and medical help immediately.
- The festival has a "Good Samaritan" policy — you won't get in trouble for seeking medical help.
- Cell service can be spotty. Establish a meet-up spot with your group at the start of each day.
- Charge your phone before arriving each day — battery drain is fast with GPS and camera use.

## Camping Tips
- Car Camping: Your car parks next to your tent. Great for storage but can be hot.
- Tent-only camping is cheaper and closer to amenities.
- Lake Eldorado is the premium camping option with pools and shade structures.
- Bring: a quality tent with stakes, a sleeping bag rated to 45°F, earplugs, a foam pad or air mattress, shower shoes, and a hanging lantern.
- Showers are available in camping areas. Arrive early in the morning to avoid long lines.
- Quiet hours are typically 2am-7am in most camping zones.

## Packing for the Budget Traveler
- Stay off-site in Indio or Palm Desert to save $300-$500 vs. on-site camping for a full weekend.
- Split an Airbnb with a group — 4-6 people sharing a house nearby is cost-effective.
- Cook your own meals at your accommodation to offset the expensive in-festival food.
- Shop at the Stater Bros. or Target in Indio before the festival for snacks, sunscreen, and supplies at retail prices.

## Weekend 1 vs Weekend 2
- Weekend 1 (April 11-13, 2025) is more hyped, more influencers, more FOMO.
- Weekend 2 (April 18-20, 2025) is often called "the locals' weekend" — calmer, same lineup, sometimes surprise guests return.
- Ticket prices are similar. Weekend 1 resale prices are significantly higher.

## The Art & Installations
- Coachella is known as much for its art as its music. Large-scale installations are scattered across the polo fields.
- Art is free to view and explore between sets.
- The Do Lab is an interactive zone with its own stage, water misters, and surprise performances — a fan favorite.
- Flower crowns and art installations make for great photo ops; explore during daylight hours.
